**Lecturer and master's supervisor, Navigation College, Dalian Maritime University**

I study safe ship navigation in complex waters. My work asks how onboard and shore-based systems can detect small objects at a distance, interpret traffic using video, Automatic Identification System (AIS) data and environmental observations, and use that information for collision avoidance, waterway monitoring and mariner training.

<h2 id="research">Research development</h2>

{% include research-overview.html %}

My doctoral work began with mariner decision-making and collision-avoidance support. I studied how to represent encounter risk and explored generative models to supplement examples of hazardous encounters. I then turned to visual perception in real navigation environments, with work on distant small targets, detector benchmarks and multisource fusion. Recent projects extend the observation area from individual ships to smart aids to navigation and shore-based monitoring, and the scale of analysis from individual encounters to waterway traffic and regional shipping-network recovery. Alongside this work, I study how ship autonomy changes bridge duties and training.

### Autonomous navigation and collision avoidance

I use relative motion, waterway constraints and changes in risk to study when ships should initiate an evasive maneuver and how a sequence of decisions can lead to actions a vessel can execute. This work includes temporal reinforcement learning for representing complex navigation states and an International Association of Maritime Universities (IAMU) project on technical challenges across degrees of autonomy in maritime autonomous surface ships (MASS).

### Maritime vision and multimodal perception

Ships and buoys can occupy very few pixels at long range and blend into the background. I study detection, tracking and visual benchmarks for these conditions. I also use video, AIS and timing information to investigate target association, missing observations and performance across waterways. Research on low-visibility navigation extends these questions to conditions such as fog.

### Smart aids to navigation, waterway monitoring and traffic resilience

I study how smart buoys can combine local observations and provide traffic information to shore authorities and remote operations centres. Related work covers estimation of buoy mooring response parameters, edge-based data fusion, augmented reality with sparse waterway data, and assessment of shipping-network disruption and recovery using AIS and emissions monitoring, reporting and verification (MRV) data.

### Maritime education and seafarer development

I study how mariners monitor automation, interpret traffic and respond when they need to take control. Shipboard IoT education and navigation simulation provide settings for designing and evaluating training exercises in encounter assessment, low-visibility watchkeeping and ship–shore communication.

### Selected projects

- Low-visibility navigation technology for ports, Shandong Key R&D Program, 2025–2028. Subproject lead.
- Ship–shore navigation assistance using smart aids to navigation, Liaoning joint science and technology programme, 2025–2027. Project lead.
- Technical challenges across degrees of autonomy in MASS, IAMU academic research project, 2024–2025. Project lead.
